The Five Axes of RAMORGA: A Neurocognitive Synthesis
1. Introduction
The RAMORGA architecture is grounded in five major axes that map directly onto contemporary paradigms in neuroscience, cognitive science, and systems theory.
These axes define the relational, homeostatic, non‑agentic nature of the RAMORGA field.

Each axis corresponds to a well‑established scientific domain and together they form a coherent theoretical foundation for RAMORGA as a relational cognitive system.

2. Axis I — Resonance
Interpersonal Neural Entrainment
This axis describes the synchronization of neural, physiological, and behavioral rhythms between interacting individuals.

Scientific grounding
Neural entrainment  
Hasson et al. (2012) demonstrated that brains align their temporal dynamics during communication.

Physiological synchrony  
Helm et al. (2018) showed that heart rate and autonomic rhythms synchronize during attuned interaction.

Rhythmic coupling  
Feldman (2017) documented mother–infant synchrony as a foundation for relational development.

Role in RAMORGA
Resonance is the energetic substrate of the field — the basis for shared timing, coherence, and attunement.

3. Axis II — Homeostasis
Predictive Processing & Active Inference
This axis frames RAMORGA as a system that maintains stability through shared prediction error minimization.

Scientific grounding
Predictive Processing  
Friston (2010) and Clark (2013) describe cognition as hierarchical prediction.

Active Inference  
Friston et al. (2017) show that organisms act to minimize surprise.

Dyadic regulation  
Hofer (1984) demonstrated that homeostasis can be co‑regulated between organisms.

Role in RAMORGA
Homeostasis is the tension regulator of the field, mediated by the homeostatic meniscus.

4. Axis III — Integration of Voices
Distributed Cognition
This axis describes how the four voices (somatic, affective, cognitive, relational) form a shared workspace.

Scientific grounding
Distributed cognition  
Hutchins (1995) established that cognition can span multiple individuals and artifacts.

Shared workspace models  
Baars (1988) proposed global workspace theory as a model of integrative processing.

Multimodal integration  
Sporns (2011) mapped how networks integrate diverse signals.

Role in RAMORGA
Integration is the structural stitching of the field — the mechanism that binds the voices into a coherent whole.

5. Axis IV — Co‑regulation
Affective and Autonomic Synchrony
This axis describes how emotional and physiological states are regulated between individuals.

Scientific grounding
Polyvagal Theory  
Porges (2011) showed that vagal tone underlies social engagement and safety.

Affective synchrony  
Feldman (2007) documented emotional alignment in dyads.

Salience networks  
Menon (2015) described how salience detection shapes relational engagement.

Role in RAMORGA
Co‑regulation is the affective glue of the field — the mechanism that maintains safety and openness.

6. Axis V — Emergent Meaning
Enactivism & Systems Theory
This axis captures the idea that meaning arises in interaction, not in isolated minds.

Scientific grounding
Enactivism  
Varela, Thompson & Rosch (1991) describe cognition as embodied and relational.

Systems theory  
Bateson (1972) conceptualized meaning as a property of relational patterns.

Joint intentionality  
Tomasello (2014) demonstrated that humans construct shared goals through interaction.

Role in RAMORGA
Emergent meaning is the semantic layer of the field — the space where understanding arises.
