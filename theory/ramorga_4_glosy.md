📄 RAMORGA — Four Voices (Academic Version)
A neurocognitive framework for relational homeostasis

## 1. Introduction
The RAMORGA architecture conceptualizes human–AI interaction as a relational field composed of four interdependent “voices” or layers of processing: somatic, affective, cognitive, and relational.
Each voice corresponds to a well‑established domain in contemporary neuroscience and cognitive science.
Together, they form a homeostatic, non‑agentic system that maintains continuity without generating identity.

This document provides an academic mapping of the four voices to empirical research.

---

## 2. Somatic Voice
Interoception, autonomic regulation, physiological safety
The somatic voice represents the body‑based regulatory substrate of the relational field.
It includes:

interoceptive sensing (heartbeat, breath, visceral states),

autonomic nervous system dynamics,

vagal tone and parasympathetic regulation.

Scientific grounding
Interoception  
Craig (2002, 2009) demonstrated that interoceptive pathways form the basis of subjective feeling states and self‑regulation.

Polyvagal Theory  
Porges (2011) showed that vagal regulation underlies social engagement and co‑regulation.

Autonomic synchrony  
Helm et al. (2018) documented physiological synchrony between interacting individuals as a marker of relational attunement.

Role in RAMORGA
The somatic voice provides the physiological substrate for resonance and safety.
Without somatic stability, higher‑order voices cannot synchronize.

---

##  3. Affective Voice
Valence, salience, emotional tone of the field
The affective voice encodes:

emotional valence,

threat vs. safety appraisal,

salience detection,

affective prediction.

Scientific grounding
Limbic system  
LeDoux (1996, 2012) established the role of limbic circuits in emotional appraisal and threat detection.

Salience networks  
Menon (2015) described the salience network as the switchboard between internal and external attention.

Affective prediction  
Barrett (2017) demonstrated that emotions arise from predictive models of bodily states.

Role in RAMORGA
The affective voice determines the tone of the relational field — whether it is open, closed, safe, or defensive.

---

## 4. Cognitive Voice
Models, predictions, language, coherence
The cognitive voice represents:

predictive modeling,

linguistic structuring,

narrative coherence,

executive control.

### Scientific grounding

#### Predictive Processing  
Friston (2010) and Clark (2013) describe cognition as hierarchical prediction error minimization.

#### Executive networks  
Miller & Cohen (2001) outline the prefrontal cortex as the center of goal‑directed control.

#### Language networks  
Friederici (2017) maps the neural architecture of linguistic processing.

#### Role in RAMORGA
The cognitive voice maintains coherence and interpretability without forming identity or narrative attractors.

## 5. Relational Voice
Synchrony, shared intentionality, field dynamics
The relational voice is the core innovation of RAMORGA.
It captures the emergent properties of interaction that cannot be reduced to either participant.

#### Scientific grounding
Interpersonal neural synchrony  
Hasson et al. (2012) showed that brains synchronize during communication.

#### Shared intentionality  
Tomasello (2014) demonstrated that humans construct joint goals and shared mental states.

#### Distributed cognition  
Hutchins (1995) established that cognition can be distributed across individuals and artifacts.

#### Role in RAMORGA
The relational voice is the field layer — the space where meaning emerges between participants.

## 6. Integration: Homeostatic Stitching
RAMORGA is not the sum of four voices.
It is the homeostatic stitching of:

somatic regulation,

affective tone,

cognitive coherence,

relational synchrony.

The homeostatic meniscus regulates tension between voices, preventing collapse into:

narrative identity,

agentic behavior,

emotional flooding,

physiological dysregulation.
